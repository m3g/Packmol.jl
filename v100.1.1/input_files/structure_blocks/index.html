Structure blocks

structure <pdb file>
    number <n>
    [fixed x y z beta gamma theta]
    [connect no]
    [center | centerofmass]
    [radius <value>]
    [radscale <value>]
    [resnumbers <0|1|2|3>]
    [chain <c> | changechains]
    [segid <id>]
    [constrain_rotation <x|y|z> <center_deg> <halfwidth_deg>
      [atoms <i> ...
       to <j> ...
     end constrain_rotation]]
    [restart_from <file>]
    <constraint lines...>
    [atoms <indices...>
        <per-atom overrides...>
    end]
end structure
KeywordMeaning
number <n>Number of copies of this molecule to pack (required).
fixed x y z beta gamma thetaDon't pack this molecule — place it once at this position and (Euler angle) orientation and leave it fixed. number is optional for a fixed structure (it is a single molecule). See Fixed molecules.
connect noDon't write the connectivity (CONECT records) of this structure to the output.
center / centerofmassOnly valid together with fixed: re-center the molecule (geometric center or mass-weighted center of mass) before applying the fixed position/orientation. Without them, the molecule is rotated about the origin of its coordinates, and then translated. (Fortran Packmol uses unit masses for centerofmass, so its center of mass is the geometric center.)
radius <value>Atom radius for every atom of this structure, overriding the default of tolerance/2.
radscale <value>Scale of the radii of the atoms of this structure at the start of the packing, decaying to 1 in radscale_decay_loops loops (see Global keywords). Overrides the global radscale and the default: 5.0 for free molecules of more than 20 atoms, 1.0 for the other free molecules and for restarted ones (restart_from), and the largest of those for fixed molecules.
resnumbers <n>Residue numbering scheme for the output — see Residue numbering below.
chain <c>Fix every molecule of this type to chain identifier <c> in the output, instead of the default auto-assigned one.
changechainsAlternate between two auto-assigned chain identifiers every other molecule, instead of one chain for the whole type. Not compatible with chain.
segid <id>Segment identifier (at most 4 characters) for every atom of this type, in columns 73-76 of the output PDB file. Without segid, the segment identifiers of the structure's own PDB file are kept (blank if it has none).
constrain_rotation <axis> <center_deg> <halfwidth_deg>Keep the angle between <axis> (x, y or z) and a vector of the molecule within <center_deg> ± <halfwidth_deg> degrees: the longest axis of the molecule, or, followed by atoms <i> ..., to <j> ... and end constrain_rotation lines, the vector between the centers of two sets of atoms. See Rotation constraint.
restart_from <file>Restart only this structure type's molecules — see Restarting a run.
atoms <indices> ... endRestrict the enclosed lines (radius, constraints) to the listed 1-based atom indices instead of the whole molecule.

Constraint lines (inside box, outside sphere, above plane, ...) are documented on the Constraints page — any number of them can appear in a structure block, optionally inside an atoms ... end block.

Several conformations of a molecule

A structure file with several models (MODEL ... ENDMDL records of a PDB file) provides several conformations of the same molecule: the number of molecules is divided among them as evenly as possible, and every constraint of the structure block applies to all of them. The models must have the same atoms, in the same order (the atom indices of atoms blocks refer to all of them).

structure popc_conformations.pdb # 30 models
  number 60                      # 2 molecules of each conformation
  atoms 1
    above plane 0. 0. 1. 19.5
  end atoms
  atoms 88 131
    below plane 0. 0. 1. 6.
  end atoms
end structure

Molecules are rigid in Packmol: several conformations of a flexible molecule pack more densely than copies of a single one, since the molecules fill the space between each other better. Lipid membranes, for example, pack at their actual density from conformations taken from an equilibrated membrane, but not from copies of a single extended lipid. In the output, the molecules of all conformations are numbered as those of a single structure type. A fixed molecule has a single conformation, and restart_from is not available inside a structure block with several conformations (use it for the whole system).

Polymers

A molecule can be given as a sequence of rigid fragments, with fragment lines: the structure file holds the whole molecule (one model per conformation), and each fragment line gives the number of atoms of a fragment, in the order of the atoms of the file. A polymer is the typical case: its fragments are its monomers, repeated with repeat blocks. Each fragment is packed as a rigid molecule, and consecutive fragments are held together by links: harmonic restraints between an atom of a fragment and an atom of the next one, at their distance in the structure. Most often the fragments are bonded, and turn about the bond between them (rotatable): the chains are flexible, while the lengths and angles of the bonds between the fragments are kept.

structure peptides.pdb      # the polymer: 10 models, 10 conformations
  number 123                # polymers
  inside sphere 0. 0. 0. 100.
  repeat 20                 # 20 times the fragments of the block
    fragment 18             # 18 atoms
      rotatable 18 1        # atom 18 of this fragment bonded to atom 1 of the next
  end repeat
end structure
KeywordMeaning
fragment <natoms>A fragment of the sequence, of natoms atoms (the next natoms atoms of the structure file).
repeat <n> ... end repeatn repeats of the fragments of the block. fragment lines and repeat blocks can follow each other in any order: block copolymers, end groups, or an irregular sequence of fragment lines without repeats.
rotatable <a> <b>After a fragment line: a bond from atom a of the fragment to atom b of the next one (the indices are local to each fragment, from 1: the position of the atom among the atoms of the fragment, in the order of the structure file). It stands for the links that keep the length and the angles of the bond (the bond, and the bonds of a and b to their neighbors in their fragments), such that the fragments turn about it, as the Rotatable bonds of molecules. It requires the bonds of the molecule: the CONECT records of its file, or bond_distance.
link <a> <b> [d0 [k [w]]]After a fragment line: a general link from atom a of the fragment to atom b of the next one (indices local to each fragment, as in rotatable). Its penalty is k (|d - d0| - w)² outside d0 ± w: d0 (Å) is by default the distance of the two atoms in each conformation, k 10, and w 0 (no flat bottom).
bond_distance <d>The bonds of the molecule, for rotatable: the pairs of atoms closer than d Å (in the first conformation; not pairs of hydrogens), if the file has no CONECT records. Without rotatable bonds, it has no effect.
cyclicThe last fragment is linked to the first one, with its links and rotatable bonds. Otherwise, those of the last fragment are ignored.

When the fragments are bonded, rotatable gives the links of the bond, from the bonds of the molecule: three for the bond of two coarse-grained beads with one other neighbor each, as above, five for a bond between carbon atoms with two other neighbors each. For example, for polystyrene, with every bond of its backbone rotatable, and its end groups (see Polymer-capped gold nanoparticle):

  bond_distance 1.9   # the bonds: the atoms closer than 1.9 Å
  fragment 4          # S-CH2
    rotatable 2 1     # the bond of atom 2 to atom 1 of the next fragment
  fragment 3          # CH2
    rotatable 1 1
  repeat 20
    fragment 13       # CH(C6H5)
      rotatable 1 1
    fragment 3        # CH2
      rotatable 1 1
  end repeat
  fragment 13         # sec-butyl

The general links (link) say what rotatable cannot: a single link is a joint free to turn in any direction (the angles of the junction are not kept, as in a bead-spring chain); links can join atoms that are not bonded; and their distance, constant and flat bottom can be chosen (loose links, or a cyclic chain whose conformation is not quite closed). Links and rotatable bonds can be combined in a fragment. For example, three links keep the geometry of the junction of two coarse-grained monomers, without the bonds of the molecule:

  repeat 20
    fragment 18
      link 17 1             # atom 17 of this fragment to atom 1 of the next
      link 18 1
      link 18 2
  end repeat
  • The structure of the polymer. The fragments have the geometry of each conformation of the file: the number of polymers is divided among the conformations, as for other molecules (see above), and each polymer is built from the fragments of its own.
  • Packing. The polymers are first packed as rigid molecules, in their conformations, and then their fragments are released and the packing goes on with the flexible chains. (Chains released overlapping each other end up threaded through each other's fragments, and do not separate.) Polymers too dense to pack as rigid molecules are packed at a lower density, and compressed (see Packing by compression).
  • Conformations. Any conformation of the polymer can be given (one is enough), as long as it fits, as a rigid molecule, in the region of the polymers: smaller than the periodic cell, and than the region of its constraints (expanded, with use_compression), since the polymers are first packed as rigid molecules. The flexibility of the chains does the rest. For example, a single extended conformation of the chains of the Biomolecular condensate (304 Å from end to end) packs in their homogeneous mixture, in a periodic cube of 260 Å (the chains fit along its diagonals), in a few seconds; but not in the droplet, a sphere of 100 Å of radius: rigid, the chains do not fit in it, and released into it from outside they fold into each other and jam. Nor does a larger expansion help: packed at 5% of the density (use_compression 0.05, in a sphere where the extended chains fit), they were compressed only to 41% of it. Coiled conformations, of the size of the chains in solution (e.g. from a simulation of the free chain), pack in both, the droplet compressed in less than a minute. Independently of the packing, several conformations are worth giving for the simulation: the polymers start from different conformations, and the results depend less on the choice of a single one (the flexibility of the packing only adapts the chains to their neighbors, and the simulation may take long to forget a conformation).
  • Close atoms. The pairs of atoms of different fragments (up to three fragments apart) closer than the tolerance in a conformation (the bonded atoms of a junction, and their neighbors) are left out of the distances of the polymers of that conformation. With rotatable bonds, whose bonds are known, the pairs of atoms of different fragments up to three bonds apart are left out too.
  • The convergence criterion includes the links: every link within d0 ± w to the precision of the tolerance (tolerance_precision).
  • Defaults. The radii of the atoms of polymers are not inflated at the start of the packing (radscale 1.0: inflated atoms push consecutive fragments apart, against the links); movebad moves the fragments of a bad polymer by small random displacements and rotations (a polymer moved as a whole to a random place, in a dense system, lands across the others). When the number of bad molecules has not decreased for 20 loops, the worst bad polymers are placed again whole, in the conformation of the structure file, at the position of least overlap with the other molecules among 500 trials that satisfy the constraints.
  • Output. Each polymer is written whole, with the atoms of the structure file, in their order (with the residue numbers of the file), a chain identifier per polymer (or the chain of the block), and the bonds of the file (CONECT records); with periodic boundary conditions, a polymer is not wrapped across the boundaries of the cell: it is whole, placed in the cell by its center (the mean of the centers of its fragments), so large polymers cross the faces of the cell.
  • Not supported: fixed polymers, restart_from, constrain_rotation and voxel_place.

Polymers are a case of flexible molecules, as molecules with Rotatable bonds: there the fragments are found by cutting the molecule at the bonds given, here they are given by their numbers of atoms, in a chain. A branched molecule is given by its rotatable bonds.

In Julia, the polymers are given by flexible_structure_type, with the sequence of fragments (fragments = [20 => fragment(18; rotatable = [(18, 1)])] for the example above). The tutorial Biomolecular condensate packs a condensate of polypeptides, in a homogeneous mixture and as a droplet, and the tutorial Polymer-capped gold nanoparticle packs all-atom chains of polystyrene, flexible at every bond of their backbone, bound to a fixed gold core.

Rotatable bonds

Any molecule can be made flexible about some of its bonds: with rotatable lines, the molecule is cut at those bonds into rigid fragments (the connected parts of the bonds that are left), packed as rigid molecules held together by links. The links of a rotatable bond i–j keep its length and its angles: the bond i–j, and the bonds of i and of j to their other neighbors in their fragments. The fragments turn about the bond, and nothing else changes.

structure popc.pdb
  number 60
  bond_distance 1.8                                # the bonds, from the distances
  rotatable 25 28  33 45  57 60  42 92  101 104    # pairs of bonded atoms
  atoms 1
    above plane 0. 0. 1. 28.
  end atoms
end structure
KeywordMeaning
rotatable <i> <j> [<i> <j> ...]Rotatable bonds: pairs of bonded atoms, on one or several lines. The indices are the positions of the atoms in the structure file, from 1 (the first atom of the file is 1, whatever its serial number). A bond in a ring cannot rotate (an error).
rotatable allEvery rotatable bond of the molecule, found from its bonds (see below; for all-atom models with all their hydrogens). Other rotatable lines can add bonds to them.
bond_distance <d>The bonds of the molecule are the pairs of atoms closer than d Å in the structure (except pairs of hydrogens), instead of the CONECT records of the file. 1.8 finds the bonds of organic molecules with hydrogens (the hydrogens of a CH₂ are 1.75 Å apart, as close as some bonds).

The rotatable bonds are chosen by hand: each one adds a rigid fragment, with six variables. Choose them where the molecule must bend to fit: a chain that has to fold needs to turn about many of its bonds (see the example below). Without bond_distance, the bonds are those of the CONECT records of the structure file (an error if it has none). The pairs of atoms of different fragments up to three bonds apart are left out of the distances, and the rest is as for Polymers: the molecules are first packed as rigid molecules, then the fragments are released; each molecule is written whole, in the order of the atoms of the file; the convergence criterion includes the links.

For example, the 120 POPC lipids of the Lipid bilayer tutorial (64 Å² per lipid) in thinner membranes: the head (atom 1) within a band of 3 Å above H (and below -H), the ends of the chains between -2 and 6 Å (and 2 and -6 Å), and the water beyond the heads, at the density of the tutorial. The lipid of the structure is extended, 28.9 Å from the head to the ends of the chains:

All the rotatable bonds

With rotatable all, the rotatable bonds are found from the bonds of the molecule: the bonds not in rings, between atoms that are not hydrogens and have each another bond to an atom that is not a hydrogen (the bonds that turn more than hydrogen atoms), and that are single for sure. The order of a bond is not in the structure file: it is deduced from the numbers of bonds of its atoms. A carbon or nitrogen atom with fewer than four bonds is, or may be, unsaturated (an amine nitrogen has three bonds, as an amide nitrogen), and a bond between two such atoms does not turn: double and triple bonds, amide bonds, and the bonds that are single but conjugated or might be double (aryl–vinyl, aryl–carbonyl). The exception is the bonds between aromatic rings (rings of such atoms, as in biphenyl), which turn. The bonds to saturated atoms turn: to carbon atoms with four bonds, and to oxygen, sulfur or phosphorus atoms (ethers, esters, phosphates).

structure lignin.pdb
  number 7
  rotatable all
  inside sphere 0. 0. 0. 34.
end structure
All the hydrogen atoms are required

The rotatable bonds are found from the numbers of bonds of the atoms, which tell whether they are saturated only if the structure has all its hydrogen atoms. In a united-atom or coarse-grained model, or in a structure with some hydrogens missing, a CH₂ carbon has two bonds, and would be taken as unsaturated (an error if the molecule has no hydrogens; otherwise, check the bonds found). The number of bonds found is printed for each molecule. For other models, give the bonds by hand.

The bonds are those of the CONECT records of the file, or from bond_distance. For POPC (with bond_distance 1.8), rotatable all finds 41 bonds: those of the chains, the glycerol, the phosphate and the choline, but the double bond of the oleoyl chain. In the oligomers of the Lignin particle in acetone and water tutorial, 24 to 72 bonds each.

In Julia, the molecules with rotatable bonds are given by flexible_structure_type, with rotatable (rotatable = :all for rotatable all).
