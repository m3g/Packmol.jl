Rotation constraint

constrain_rotation <x|y|z> <center_deg> <halfwidth_deg>

or, with the vector of the molecule given by two sets of atoms:

constrain_rotation <x|y|z> <center_deg> <halfwidth_deg>
  atoms <i> <j> ...
  to <k> <l> ...
end constrain_rotation

Unlike the shape constraints above, this does not restrict where a structure's atoms are placed, but how the molecule is oriented: the angle between the given axis and a vector of the molecule must be within <center_deg> ± <halfwidth_deg> degrees. The vector goes from the center of the atoms given in the atoms line to the center of those given in the to line (indices of the atoms in the structure file). For example, to keep the vector from the head (atoms 1 and 2) to the tail (atom 42) of a lipid within 15° of the z axis:

constrain_rotation z 0. 15.
  atoms 1 2
  to 42
end constrain_rotation

Without the sets of atoms, the vector is the longest axis of the molecule (its principal axis of inertia with the smallest moment, with all atoms with the same mass). Its sign is arbitrary, so use the sets of atoms when the direction matters (center 90 constrains it to a plane, which doesn't depend on the sign).

The constraint is a penalty, like the others: the squared displacement of the tip of the vector, outside the allowed cone. Repeat the line (or block) to constrain the angles with more than one axis. In the Julia API, it is the constrain_rotation keyword of structure_type.

Difference from Fortran Packmol

Fortran Packmol bounds three Euler angles of the molecule, relative to its orientation in the structure file. The same constrain_rotation lines give different orientations here. For instance, Fortran's constrain_rotation x 0 10 alone bounds the tilt of the z axis of the molecule (as given in its file), while here it keeps the longest axis of the molecule within 10° of x.

Example

40 octane molecules in a box, with their longest axis within 10° of the z axis:

structure octane.pdb
    number 40
    inside box 0. 0. 0. 30. 30. 30.
    constrain_rotation z 0. 10.
end structure

The same system, packed with the Julia API and shown with the visualize function of PDBTools.jl:

using Packmol, PDBToolsoctane = structure_type("octane.pdb";    number = 40,    constraints = [InsideBox([0., 0., 0.], [30., 30., 30.])],    constrain_rotation = Dict(:z => (0.0, 10.0)),)system = PackmolSystem([octane]; tolerance = 2.0, output = "rotation.pdb")packmol(system)visualize(get_atoms(system); unitcell = UnitCell([30., 30., 30.]))
