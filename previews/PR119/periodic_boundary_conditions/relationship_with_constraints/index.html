Relationship with constraints

Once pbc/unitcell is set, it changes how every distance in the system is computed, and how every explicit constraint (declared inside a structure ... end structure block, or via Inside/Outside constructors in the Julia API) is evaluated — on top of adding one implicit constraint of its own.

Interatomic distances. With PBC active, the tolerance/overlap distance between two atoms is always the minimum-image distance: each molecule's center of mass is wrapped to the periodic image nearest the cell's center (unitcell_center) before distances are computed, so a molecule near one face of the cell interacts naturally with molecules near the opposite face, as if the cell tiled space. A molecule is always wrapped as a rigid unit, never atom by atom, so it can never be torn across a periodic boundary in a way that would leave part of it overlapping unrelated atoms.

The shape of the cell. For orthorhombic and general triclinic cells, the molecules are wrapped into the parallelepiped of the cell vectors (the one unitcell/CRYST1 describes), centered at unitcell_center. Dodecahedral and octahedral cells (pbc dodecahedral/pbc octahedral, or a unitcell built with dodecahedral_unitcell/octahedral_unitcell) are, instead, the rhombic dodecahedron and the truncated octahedron centered at unitcell_center: the molecules are wrapped to their image closest to the center, which is in that shape. This is what the constraints of these cells refer to, and it is independent of periodic_boundary_style, which only affects how get_atoms/write_output present the final coordinates (see the output shapes of the rhombic dodecahedral and truncated octahedral boxes). It matters for constraints on large regions: the sphere inscribed in a truncated octahedron of size d has radius d/2, while the one inscribed in the parallelepiped of its cell vectors has radius 0.408 d, so, for example, outside sphere constraints around a solute (as the water around a vesicle) can use nearly all the cell.

Explicit constraints. Any inside/outside/above/below constraint declared for a structure is checked against that same wrapped position — so, for example, inside sphere cx cy cz r together with PBC confines a molecule to a sphere drawn in the wrapped cell, not in unwrapped/raw coordinate space.

The centers of mass in the cell. Minimum-image distances and explicit constraints, by themselves, only bound how atoms interact given a position: neither one stops the center of mass of a molecule from drifting along a direction that nothing else bounds. This is most visible for a half-space constraint like above plane/below plane, which has no periodicity of its own: a molecule above a plane could wander arbitrarily far along the plane. So, under periodic boundary conditions, the optimizer bounds the centers of mass of the molecules to the cell (to the box that contains it, for dodecahedral and octahedral cells), in the packing and in the fits of the initial placement, and the centers of mass are kept at their images in the cell. There is no need to add to every structure an inside box matching the cell. The atoms of a molecule are not confined: a molecule near a face of the cell crosses it, and interacts with the molecules on the other side through the periodic boundary.
