Free volume

The free volume calculator reads a Packmol input file and estimates, by Monte Carlo, the volume of the region available to the molecules of each structure type: the fraction of random points of the periodic cell (or of the box of its inside constraints) that satisfy the constraints of the structure type and are farther than the tolerance from the atoms of the fixed molecules. With it comes the density at which the molecules of the structure type fill their region, which shows when an input asks for too many molecules (the packing is then slow, or fails) or too few. The densities of structure types that share a region add up. Nothing leaves the page: the files are read by the browser.

Paste the input file, or open it, and select its structure files: the files of the fixed molecules are needed to take their volume out of the regions, and those of the free molecules give the densities in g/mL (without them, the densities are given in molecules per nm³).

The region is that of the constraints that apply to the whole molecule. The constraints of some atoms only (in atoms blocks) are ignored: the volume is then an upper bound, and a structure type with constraints of some atoms only (as the lipids of a membrane, whose heads and tails are above and below planes) has no estimate. Neither has one a structure type whose region is not bounded, by the periodic cell or by an inside box, cube, sphere, cylinder or ellipsoid. The constraints of Packmol.jl without an equivalent in Fortran Packmol (Gaussian, wave and exponential surfaces) are not supported here.

The same estimate is given by free_volume in Julia, and by packmol --free-volume input.inp, which read the structure files of the input (see Volume estimates).
