Custom constraints

CustomConstraint(f; gradient = nothing, weight = 5.0)

A region defined by a function of the position: the atoms are kept where f(x) ≤ 0, for x the position of an atom (an SVector{3}, in Å). Any region that can be written as an inequality can be used, combined with the other constraints of the molecule as usual. This constraint is available only in Julia code, not in input files or in the packmol executable.

Outside the region, the penalty is weight * f(x)^2, so f should grow with the distance to the region. A signed distance is the best choice (as f(x) = norm(x) - R, for a sphere of radius R), but any function that is positive outside the region, and grows smoothly away from it, works. The packing also needs the gradient of f:

  • if gradient is not given, it is computed by central finite differences (six evaluations of f for each atom outside the region);
  • it can be computed exactly by automatic differentiation, for example with ForwardDiff.jl: gradient = x -> ForwardDiff.gradient(f, x);
  • or it can be written by hand, for a function with a simple gradient.

CustomConstraint(f; gradient = nothing, weight = 5.0, T = Float64)

A constraint defined by a function of the position: the atoms are kept in the region where f(x) ≤ 0, for x the position of an atom (an SVector{3}, in Å). Outside the region, the penalty is weight * f(x)^2, so f should grow with the distance to the region (as f(x) = norm(x) - R, for a sphere of radius R). Write f in Å, not without units (as (norm(x)/R)^2 - 1): for large regions, the gradient of such a function at small violations is below the stopping tolerance of the optimizer, and the fits of the initial placement do not converge.

gradient(x) is the gradient of f at x. If it is not given, it is computed by central finite differences (6 evaluations of f). It can be computed exactly by automatic differentiation, for example with gradient = x -> ForwardDiff.gradient(f, x).

It is a constraint of whole molecules (constraints) or of some of their atoms (atom_constraints), as the other constraints. There is no input file syntax for it.

using Packmol, LinearAlgebra# Inside a torus of radii 20 and 6 Å around the z axistorus = CustomConstraint(x -> hypot(hypot(x[1], x[2]) - 20, x[3]) - 6)water = structure_type("water.pdb"; number = 500, constraints = [torus])
source

The scale of f

Write f in Å, as a distance, not as a ratio without units. The fits of the initial placement push every molecule until its penalty is practically zero (below 1e-10), and the optimizer stops when the gradient of the penalty, 2 weight f(x) ∇f(x), falls below 1e-5. With |∇f| ≈ 1 (a distance), that happens at violations of about 1e-6 Å, where the penalty is negligible. With a function without units, |∇f| may be tiny: for f(x) = (norm(x)/R)^2 - 1, the inside of a sphere of radius R, it is 2/R, and with R = 320 Å the optimizer stops with the atoms still about 0.01 Å outside, while their penalties are above 1e-10. The fits then run all their loops without converging, as packing 20,000 lipids in a shell inside such a sphere shows:

f(x)units of fabs(∇f) at the surfacefirst fit
norm(x) - RÅ1converged in 0.4 s
sum(abs2, x) - R^2Å²2Rconverged in 0.6 s
sum(abs2, x) / R^2 - 1none2/Rnot converged after 200 loops

So:

  • prefer a signed distance, or a function with a gradient of about 1 near the boundary of the region (a function that grows faster, as sum(abs2, x) - R^2, also works near the region, but far from it the penalty grows as the fourth power of the distance: it dominates the distances between atoms in the objective function, and atoms placed hundreds of Å out of a large region make the first steps of the optimizer huge);
  • do not divide f by the size of the region: scale a function given without units by a length of the region (the inside of an ellipsoid of semi-axes a, b, c is m (ρ - 1), for ρ = norm(x ./ (a, b, c)) and m = min(a, b, c), as the Ellipsoid constraint);
  • or, which has the same effect, increase weight (by the square of that length).

The gradient can be checked with ForwardDiff.jl, comparing ForwardDiff.gradient(x -> Packmol.constraint_penalty(c, x), x) to Packmol.constraint_gradient(c, x) at points outside the region.

Example

Water in a torus around the z axis, with radii of 14 and 6 Å (the distance between the center of the tube and the axis, and the radius of the tube). The distance of a point to the circle of the center of the tube, minus the radius of the tube, is the signed distance to the surface of the torus. The torus has a volume of about 9950 Å³, which holds 330 water molecules at 1 g/mL:

using Packmol, PDBTools, ForwardDifftorus(x) = hypot(hypot(x[1], x[2]) - 14, x[3]) - 6water = structure_type("water.pdb";    number = 330,    constraints = [        CustomConstraint(torus;            gradient = x -> ForwardDiff.gradient(torus, x)),    ],)system = PackmolSystem([water]; tolerance = 2.0, output = "torus.pdb")packmol(system)visualize(get_atoms(system);    rotate = [(:z, 30), (:x, -70)],)

Custom constraints can also be applied to some atoms of each molecule, with atom_constraints, as the other constraints (see Constraints on some atoms).
