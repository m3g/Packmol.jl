Placement on a grid (Bentopy)

A port of Bentopy's algorithm

The function on this page is a direct port to Julia of the placement algorithm of Bentopy (its packing step, bentopy-pack), by Westendorp, Stevens, Brown, Dommer, Wassenaar, Bruininks and Marrink:

M. S. S. Westendorp et al., Compartment-guided assembly of large-scale molecular models with Bentopy, Protein Science (2026) e70480. doi:10.1002/pro.70480

Bentopy builds models of whole cells, tens of thousands of proteins, RNAs and metabolites, in minutes, by placing the molecules one after the other on an occupancy grid, with no optimization at all. Packing its published model of the JCVI-Syn3A minimal cell is what showed us how well this works as a starting point for Packmol, and what led to voxel_place. The algorithm, and its effectiveness, are Bentopy's: if you use voxel_place, please cite Bentopy.

What it does

voxel_place, the Julia port of Bentopy's packing algorithm, takes a system (or an input file), places its free molecules as Bentopy does, and drops the molecules that do not fit:

  • The space is a grid of voxels (of side resolution): the periodic cell or, without periodic boundary conditions, the box of the regions of the molecules. The voxels taken by the fixed molecules are marked.
  • Each molecule is voxelized conservatively at a random rotation: the voxels that intersect a sphere of the radius of each of its atoms (Bentopy's bead radius). Two molecules that do not share voxels are therefore at distances larger than the sum of the radii of their atoms: the tolerance.
  • The compartment of each type (Bentopy's masks) is the set of voxels whose corners satisfy the constraints of the type.
  • The types are placed in decreasing order of their moment (the sum of the squared distances of the atoms to their center), each type completely before the next.
  • Each try puts the molecule at a random free voxel of its compartment, and accepts it if none of its voxels is taken. The rotation changes after each placement, and after max_tries_mult × number ÷ max_tries_rot_div unsuccessful tries; the type gives up after max_tries_mult × number unsuccessful tries (defaults 1000 and 100, as in Bentopy), and the molecules that were not placed are dropped.
using Packmolplaced, dropped = voxel_place("cell.inp")   # or voxel_place(system)packmol(placed)

In an input file, the keyword voxel_place (optionally followed by max_tries_mult) does the same within packmol, also from the command line: the free molecules are placed by voxel_place, and the system of those that fit is packed.

tolerance 4.0
voxel_place 100000
...

The molecules that do not fit are reported with a warning, and with a significant warning when more than 10% of the molecules of a structure type are left out. The voxel placement puts rigid molecules at random positions and rotations, and can fall far short of the space available: for the lipids of the Zika virion membrane (heads and tails constrained to opposite sides of a surface), it placed a quarter to a half of the lipids that the volume of the leaflets (free_volume) could hold. Compare the numbers with the volume available before relying on the placement, and pack without it in such cases.

placed is a system with the molecules that fit, which starts from their positions (placed.restart_from, see Restart files), and dropped is the number of molecules of each structure type that did not fit (the types are listed at the end of the placement). The composition of placed is not that of the input when some molecules do not fit: check it before using the result, and enlarge the region or reduce the numbers if the difference matters.

The resolution is by default 2.5 times the largest radius of the atoms of the free molecules (Bentopy's models use voxels of 5 Å and beads of 2 Å radius: a Packmol tolerance of 4 Å). A radius keyword sets the same radius for all atoms, as Bentopy's bead radius.

The port takes the regions of the molecules from Packmol's constraints instead of Bentopy's compartment files, which asks for a few adaptations: constraints that apply to some atoms of a molecule only, and orientation constraints, are checked on the atoms of a try that does not collide; without periodic boundary conditions each type needs a constraint that bounds its region (inside a box, cube, sphere, ellipsoid or cylinder); periodic cells must be orthorhombic. It works in 3D only.

When to use it

For crowded systems of many large molecules, where the usual initial placement leaves large molecules interpenetrated and the packing stalls with a few of them that cannot find room. On Bentopy's model of the JCVI-Syn3A minimal cell (the 41,967 proteins, RNAs and metabolites of Bentopy's input, 16 million coarse-grained beads, inside a sphere of 950 Å, with the membrane and the chromosome fixed, tolerance 4 Å; see the Minimal cell tutorial):

  • the usual initial placement did not converge in 40 loops;
  • voxel_place (with Bentopy's max_tries_mult = 100_000 of this model) placed 41,835 molecules in 16 seconds, leaving out 132 RNAs, as Bentopy did, and the packing from it converged at the first loop. Solvating it then (17.3 million water beads and 3 million glycerol placeholders, the cell fixed, tolerance 2 Å) took 2 loops: the whole cell built in about 11 minutes.

On a part of the same cell (3,122 molecules in a sphere of 400 Å), Bentopy and voxel_place placed the same molecules (both dropped the same RNA) in a few seconds, and the packings from both placements converged at the first loop.

For solvents and dilute solutions it is not needed: the usual initial placement and the packing are fast there.

Bentopy and Packmol together

Bentopy itself is the tool to build models of cells and other crowded compartments: compartments from voxel masks and combinations of them, concentrations, and the features its authors keep adding. Check its repository for what it offers before building such a system by other means. Its output can be taken by Packmol, for what Packmol adds:

  • Removing the remaining overlaps at a given tolerance: restart from Bentopy's coordinates (restart_from, a coordinate file with the atoms in the order of the structure blocks of the input, each structure block a molecule type of Bentopy's model). On the minimal cell this took 2 loops (94 s).
  • Solvation, with any solvent and mixtures, the molecules of the model fixed: the minimal cell was solvated with 17.3 million water beads and 3 million glycerol placeholders in 5.4 minutes at a tolerance of 2 Å.
  • Constraints that Bentopy does not express: regions of any shape (planes, curved surfaces, surfaces given by points), constraints on some atoms of a molecule, and orientations.

Bentopy, like voxel_place, leaves out the molecules that do not fit (on the minimal cell, 132 of 686 RNAs). Whether Packmol can add them to the placed system, the others free to move, is an open question.
