Packing by compression

Some systems do not pack from a random placement at their density: long chains, polymers, crowded large molecules. The molecules jam before they fill the space, each blocked by its neighbors, while the same molecules pack in seconds at a lower density. The keyword use_compression packs such systems at a lower density, and compresses them from there, in steps, to the density of the input:

use_compression       # pack at half the density, then compress

The input describes the system at its density (the constraints, the periodic cell, the numbers of molecules), and use_compression [<fraction> [<step>]] takes two optional values:

  • fraction (default 0.5): the fraction of that density at which the system is first packed. With 0.5, the system is packed in twice its volume, at half the density; with 0.3, at 30% of it.
  • step (default 0.05): the initial step of the compression, the fraction by which the linear dimensions shrink at each step (5%).

use_compression alone is use_compression 0.5 0.05:

  1. The constraints and the periodic cell are expanded by fraction^(-1/3) (by 1.26 for 0.5), about the center of the cell (the origin, without periodic boundary conditions), and the system is packed there. If the packing at that density does not converge, the geometry is expanded further (to half the density, the molecules with it) and the system packed again from there, up to three times.
  2. At each step, the geometry is scaled by 1 - step about the same center: the constraints, the cell, and the positions of the free molecules, each moved as a whole (the center of mass of a molecule is scaled; a flexible molecule, a polymer or a molecule with rotatable bonds, moves with the center of its fragments), and the fixed molecules, scaled with the radii of their atoms (see below). The system is packed again, from there, for up to 100 loops.
  3. When a step does not converge, the system breathes: the packing of the failed step is expanded to one step below the last density that converged, and packed there (a relaxation), keeping the rearrangements the failed step made. The compression resumes from the relaxed packing, with half the step (if the relaxation does not converge either, or after 20 relaxations, it resumes from the last converged packing). After two steps that converge, the step grows by 1.5, up to the initial step. The compression ends at the geometry of the input, or stops (with a warning, the output at the density reached) if a step smaller than 0.0005 does not converge.

The report shows each step:

  Compression step    1: density   58.32% of the target, converged (0.8 s)
  Compression step    2: density   68.02% of the target, converged (1.5 s)
  Compression step    3: density   79.33% of the target, converged (3.0 s)
  Compression step    4: density   92.53% of the target, not converged (7.6 s)
  Relaxation    1:        density   68.02% of the target, converged (0.1 s)
  Compression step    5: density   73.39% of the target, converged (0.1 s)
  ...
  Compression step   15: density  100.00% of the target, converged (1.9 s)

  Compressed to the density of the input in 15 steps (28.0 s).

Undoing a failed step and halving it, without relaxing it, stopped the compression of the particle of the Lignin particle in acetone and water tutorial short of the density in most runs: the next steps failed at the same configuration. Relaxing at every failed step, it reached the density in all runs (5 of 5 seeds), while relaxing only as a last resort, once the step had shrunk below its minimum, failed in one of five and took 1.6 times longer.

The compression works with rigid molecules and with flexible ones: Polymers and molecules with Rotatable bonds. With flexible molecules it goes much further, as they change their shape to fill the space: the chains of the condensate of the tutorial Biomolecular condensate, packed as rigid molecules, jam at about 220 mg/mL, while as flexible chains they are compressed to 400 mg/mL in about a minute; the lignin oligomers of the Lignin particle in acetone and water, flexible at every rotatable bond (rotatable all), reach the density of lignin, 1.30 g/cm³. Large steps push the flexible molecules into each other, and leave them entangled (chains threaded through the fragments of others), hence the adaptive step and the relaxations.

In Julia, use_compression is a keyword argument of packmol and of PackmolSystem: use_compression = true (the defaults), use_compression = 0.3 (the fraction), or use_compression = (0.3, 0.02) (the fraction and the initial step).

Periodic cells and fixed molecules

All periodic cells are supported (orthorhombic, triclinic, dodecahedral, octahedral): they are scaled uniformly about their center, which keeps their shape.

Fixed molecules are supported, and are scaled with the geometry: the coordinates of their atoms about the center of the scaling, and the radii of their atoms, such that the constraints placed relative to them keep their relation to them. At the end of the compression, they are back at the coordinates and with the radii of the input. For example, the Zika virion (a large system), packed with use_compression 0.5: its membrane, in spherical shells chosen to fit inside the fixed shell of proteins, crossed by their transmembrane segments, swells and shrinks with the proteins, and the virion is compressed to its density in 5 steps (in about 2 minutes, while it packs without compression in about one). With the proteins kept at their place, the shells of the membrane, expanded, were beyond them, and the compression had not gone beyond a third of the density after 25 minutes.

Fixed molecules, scaled

A fixed molecule is scaled as a whole: its atoms move apart, and grow, by the same factor (by 1.26 for use_compression 0.5). The free molecules around it are not, so in the expanded geometry they meet a larger version of it: free molecules placed in its cavities or grooves (water inside a protein, ligands in a pocket, the lipids crossed by a transmembrane helix) see them as they are only at the end of the compression, and may need the last steps to settle. This works when the free molecules are around the fixed ones, or in regions whose shapes follow them; it is not known to work in every case. If the compression stops short of the density, the output is written at the density reached, the fixed molecules scaled with it.

Limitations
  • The constraints must be boxes, cubes, spheres, cylinders, ellipsoids or planes (the others cannot be scaled).
  • Not with restart_from, nor with voxel_place.
