Packmol.jl

Packmol creates initial configurations for molecular dynamics simulations, by packing molecules in regions of space defined by geometric constraints, such that the atoms of different molecules are kept at a safe distance from each other.

Packmol.jl is the new implementation of Packmol. It reads the input files of the legacy (Fortran) Packmol and runs them with the same command line, and it can also be used from Julia code, where systems are defined without input files and can be built from target densities and concentrations (see Recipes). It uses CellListMap.jl for the computation of distances and SPGBox.jl as the optimizer.

New in Packmol.jl

Relative to the legacy (Fortran) Packmol:

  • Much faster: 2 to 16 times faster in a single thread, and multithreaded, up to about 45 times faster on a multicore computer (see the table below). Large systems are packed in seconds or minutes: nanoliposomes of 350 and 600 thousand atoms in 16 and 31 seconds, a whole Zika virion (2.2 million particles) in 47 seconds, and an exosome of 13.6 million atoms in 33 minutes, with 14 threads.
  • Periodic boundary conditions in orthorhombic, triclinic, rhombic dodecahedral and truncated octahedral cells.
  • New constraints: curved surfaces (Gaussian, waves, exponentials), and constrain_rotation for the angle between an axis and a vector of the molecule (to build oriented phases, such as liquid crystals).
  • mmCIF files, for structures, outputs and restarts, and restarts from the output of a previous run.
  • Several conformations of a molecule, from a structure file with several models.
  • Volume estimates, to compute the numbers of molecules (--guess-volume, --free-volume), and check_initial_placement, to check the constraints before packing.
  • A Julia API: systems defined without input files, recipes for solvated systems and membranes built from target densities and concentrations, user-defined constraints (CustomConstraint), and the packed atoms in memory, for analysis with PDBTools.jl.

Packing times of the examples of the legacy Packmol (seconds; the executable of Packmol.jl, and of Packmol 21.1.4, on an Intel Core i9-14900KF):

InputLegacy PackmolPackmol.jl, 1 threadPackmol.jl, 14 threads
water and urea (mixture)1.40.120.11
solvated protein (solvprotein)13.10.810.37
lipid bilayer (bilayer)26.12.30.57
spherical vesicle (spherical)36.317.42.6

The input files of the legacy Packmol run unchanged, with few exceptions: see Migrating from legacy Packmol.

Documentation

  • Migrating from legacy Packmol: what changed from the Fortran Packmol, what is no longer supported, and what is new.
  • Tutorials: complete examples, each with an input file for the command line and the same system defined in Julia.
  • Input files: the global keywords and the structure ... end structure blocks.
  • Constraints: the geometric regions (box, sphere, plane, cylinder, ellipsoid, ...) in which molecules, or some of their atoms, are packed.
  • Periodic boundary conditions: orthorhombic, triclinic, rhombic dodecahedral and truncated octahedral cells.
  • Defining systems in Julia: building and packing a system with structure_type, PackmolSystem and packmol, without an input file.
  • Recipes: solvated systems and membranes built from target densities and concentrations, instead of numbers of molecules.
  • Concentration units: the conversions between concentration units (cconvert) used by the recipes.
  • Reference: the docstrings of the public functions.

About the logo

The logo is an image of the Zika virion built with Packmol.jl: a complete virion of the Zika virus, in a coarse-grained model, with its protein shell, a membrane of two layers of lipids beneath it, and water and ions inside and around it, in a rhombic dodecahedron periodic cell. The system has 2.2 million particles, and it is packed in a few minutes.
