Volume estimates

Three estimates help to choose the numbers of molecules, or the size of the box, of an input: the volume that the molecules fill at a given density, the volume of the region available to the molecules of each structure type, given its constraints and the fixed molecules, and the volume of the molecules themselves. None packs the system. From the command line, with the input file:

packmol --guess-volume input.inp
packmol --free-volume input.inp
packmol --molecular-volume input.inp

and in Julia, with guess_volume, free_volume and molecular_volume, which print the same summaries. The first two are also online calculators, in the browser: the volume guesser and the free volume calculator, at the top of the Recipes tab.

The volume of the molecules at a density

The volume of the box, and the side of a cube of that volume: the mass of the free molecules divided by the density, as the volume guesser of the Packmol web page, plus the volume of the fixed molecules, their molecular volume (as the solvation recipes count the volume of the solute).

From the command line, --density sets the density (g/mL, 1.0 by default):

packmol --guess-volume --density 1.09 input.inp

In Julia, guess_volume takes an input file, a PackmolSystem, or structure files or molar masses (g/mol) with their numbers of molecules, and the density keyword. For the water and urea tutorial (1000 water and 400 urea molecules), at 1.09 g/mL:

using Packmolguess_volume("mixture/mixture.inp"; density = 1.09)

  Volume guess, at a density of 1.09 g/mL:

    water.pdb                          1000 molecules of 18.02 g/mol
    urea.pdb                            400 molecules of 60.06 g/mol

  Total mass:   42038 g/mol
  Volume:       64041 Å³
  Side of cube: 40.01 Å

which is the cube of 40 Å of the input. The same, with the molecules given directly (all free):

water = "mixture/water.pdb"guess_volume([water => 1000, 60.06 => 400]; density = 1.09)

  Volume guess, at a density of 1.09 g/mL:

    water.pdb                          1000 molecules of 18.02 g/mol
    60.06 g/mol                         400 molecules of 60.06 g/mol

  Total mass:   42039 g/mol
  Volume:       64044 Å³
  Side of cube: 40.01 Å

For the solvated protein, the protein is fixed, and counts with its molecular volume:

guess_volume("solvprotein/solvprotein.inp")

  Volume guess, at a density of 1.0 g/mL:

    thyroid_receptor.pdb                  1 molecules of 30284 g/mol, fixed: 34947 Å³
    water.pdb                         35480 molecules of 18.02 g/mol
    CLA.pdb                              96 molecules of 35.45 g/mol
    SOD.pdb                             106 molecules of 22.99 g/mol

  Total mass:   675309 g/mol
  Volume of the fixed molecules: 34947 Å³ (molecular volume)
  Volume:       1106036 Å³
  Side of cube: 103.42 Å

The volume available to each structure type

The volume of the region of each structure type, by Monte Carlo: the fraction of random points of a box that contains the region that satisfy its constraints and are farther than the tolerance from the atoms of the fixed molecules. With it comes the density at which the molecules of the structure type fill their region: much above the density of the liquid, there are too many molecules for the region (and the packing will be slow, or fail); much below, too few.

The region is that of the constraints that apply to the whole molecule. The constraints of some atoms only (in atoms blocks) are ignored, so that the volume is an upper bound, and a structure type with constraints of some atoms only has no estimate. A structure type whose region is not bounded (without periodic boundary conditions, by an inside box, cube, sphere, cylinder or ellipsoid) has no estimate either. The precision of the estimate is given by its standard error, which decreases with the square root of the number of points.

From the command line, --npoints sets the number of points (1 000 000 by default):

packmol --free-volume --npoints 500000 input.inp

In Julia, free_volume takes an input file or a PackmolSystem, and the npoints keyword. For the solvated protein, the water, sodium and chloride share the rhombic dodecahedron, without the volume of the protein:

input = "solvprotein/solvprotein.inp"free_volume(input; npoints = 500_000)

  Volume available to each structure type (Monte Carlo, 500000 points):

    Structure                 Number     Volume (Å³)     ± error  Density (g/mL)
    water.pdb                  35480         1061134      2879.0             1.0
    CLA.pdb                       96         1057648      2875.6           0.005
    SOD.pdb                      106         1062445      2880.3           0.004

  The density is that at which the molecules of each structure type fill its region.

The water fills its region at 1 g/mL. The densities of structure types that share a region add up: the water and the urea of the mixture above, in the same box, are at 0.47 and 0.62 g/mL, 1.09 g/mL together. In the lipid bilayer, the heads and the ends of the chains of the lipids are above and below planes (constraints of some atoms), which do not bound a region of the molecules; the water around them has its own region:

free_volume("bilayer/bilayer.inp"; npoints = 500_000)

  Volume available to each structure type (Monte Carlo, 500000 points):

    Structure                 Number     Volume (Å³)     ± error  Density (g/mL)
    popc.pdb                      60               -           -               -   region set by constraints of some atoms only
    popc.pdb                      60               -           -               -   region set by constraints of some atoms only
    water.pdb                   1925           57321       181.8           1.005
    water.pdb                   1925           57460       181.9           1.002

  The density is that at which the molecules of each structure type fill its region.

The volume of the molecules

The volume of each molecule, as the space that a spherical probe cannot enter, computed on a grid as by MoloVol. With the default probe (1.4 Å, a water molecule), it is the volume that a solute takes from the solvent: for a protein, close to its volume in water, while its mass at 1 g/mL gives about 1.4 times that. It is the volume of the fixed molecules in the volume at a density, and that of the solute in the solvation recipes. For small molecules, as the solvent, the volume in the liquid is larger (it includes the empty space between the molecules): the volume at a density is the one for them.

The result lists, for each structure of the input file, the volume of one molecule and of all its molecules, and the total. From the command line:

packmol --molecular-volume input.inp

--probe sets the radius of the probe (Å, 1.4 by default; 0 gives the van der Waals volume), and --resolution the spacing of the grid (Å, 0.4 by default, at which the volume is within about 1.5% of its converged value).

In Julia, molecular_volume takes an input file, a PackmolSystem, or structure files with their numbers of molecules, and the probe and resolution keywords. For the solvated protein:

molecular_volume("solvprotein/solvprotein.inp")

  Molecular volumes (probe of 1.4 Å, resolution of 0.4 Å):

    Structure                        Number     Volume (Å³)      Total (Å³)
    thyroid_receptor.pdb                  1           34947           34947
    water.pdb                         35480            17.9          635802
    CLA.pdb                              96            23.4          2242.6
    SOD.pdb                             106            49.6          5257.6

  Total volume: 678249 Å³

It also gives the volume of a single molecule, from its atoms:

using PDBToolsmolecular_volume(read_pdb("solvprotein/thyroid_receptor.pdb"))

The volume (Å³) of a system, and the side (Å) of a cube of that volume: the volume that the free molecules fill at the density (g/mL), as the volume guesser of the Packmol web page, and the volume of the fixed molecules, their molecular volume (see molecular_volume), as the solvation recipes count the volume of the solute. Packmol.free_volume — Function
free_volume(input_file::String; npoints = 1_000_000, seed = 1)
free_volume(system::PackmolSystem; npoints = 1_000_000, seed = 1)

The volume of the region available to each structure type, by Monte Carlo.

The volume (Å³) of the region of the molecules of each structure type of an input file or a PackmolSystem is estimated from npoints random points of a box that contains the region: the fraction of them that satisfies the constraints of the structure type and is farther than the tolerance from the atoms of the fixed molecules. With the volume, the density at which the molecules of the structure type would fill the region (g/mL), which shows when too many (or too few) molecules were asked for.

The region is that of the constraints that apply to whole molecules. Those of some atoms only (in atoms blocks) are ignored: the volume is then an upper bound, and a structure type with constraints of some atoms only (as the lipids of a membrane, with their heads and tails above and below planes) has no estimate. Neither has one a structure type whose region is not bounded, by the periodic cell or by an inside box, cube, sphere, cylinder or ellipsoid.

Returns a vector with, for each structure type, (; structure, number, volume, error, density, note): the error is the standard error of the estimate (Å³), and volume, error and density are NaN without an estimate (note says why). Prints a summary.

Packmol.molecular_volume — Function
molecular_volume(atoms::AbstractVector{<:Atom}; probe = 1.4, resolution = 0.4)
molecular_volume(input_file::String; probe = 1.4, resolution = 0.4)
molecular_volume(system::PackmolSystem; probe = 1.4, resolution = 0.4)
molecular_volume(molecules::AbstractVector{<:Pair}; probe = 1.4, resolution = 0.4)

The molecular volume (Å³): the volume from which a spherical probe of radius probe (Å) is excluded by the van der Waals spheres of the atoms (the van der Waals volume and the gaps between the atoms where the probe does not fit), computed on a grid of spacing resolution (Å), as by MoloVol. Cavities where the probe fits are not part of the volume. With probe = 0, the van der Waals volume.

With the default probe (the radius of a water molecule), the molecular volume of a protein is close to its volume in water (about 0.73 mL/g). The radii are the van der Waals radii of the elements, so that the volume is that of all-atom structures. At the default resolution, the volume is within about 1.5% of its converged value; a smaller one is more precise, at a cost that grows as its inverse cube.

Of the atoms of a molecule, returns its volume. Of the structure types of an input file or of a PackmolSystem, or of pairs of structure files and numbers of molecules (as ["protein.pdb" => 1, "water.pdb" => 1000]), returns (; volume, molecules): the total volume of the molecules, and for each structure type (; structure, number, volume), the volume of one molecule. Prints a summary.

Example

using Packmol, PDBToolsmolecular_volume(read_pdb("protein.pdb"))
